# Transit research prototype (not a released app)

This is a small, deterministic Moon-sign forecast engine. It computes Lahiri sidereal positions with Swiss Ephemeris's built-in Moshier model, then emits **only** enabled rules whose original PDF pages were checked visually. It cannot reproduce Balaji Haasan's full consultations. It does not generate interpretation with a language model. Read the [public benchmark](../docs/balaji-haasan-public-technique-benchmark.md) before using it.

## License gate

Swiss Ephemeris is dual-licensed: AGPL or a paid professional license. Its owner says the choice must be made before distributing software using it or activating a public service: https://www.astro.com/swisseph/sweph_e.htm . `pyswisseph` binds this library. This code is research-only until the product's compatible license or professional license is decided. No ephemeris binary is committed. Review the edition/scan rights in `books.json` too. Historical astrology is not scientifically validated; outputs are not health, finance or life advice.

## Try locally (Python 3.10+)

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv
. .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r engine/requirements-research.txt
python3 -m engine.forecast --date 2026-01-01 --moon-sign Aquarius
python3 -m engine.forecast --date 2026-01-01 --birth-date 2000-01-01 --birth-time 14:30 --birth-tz Asia/Kolkata
python3 -m engine.benchmark
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v
```

`--date` samples midnight UTC, not local sunrise. A known Moon sign allows like-for-like comparison with public rashi videos. With a person's birth data, date, **real** clock time and IANA timezone are mandatory; never invent a birth time. Birth place is not needed for the Moon-sign-only slice; the optional natal ascendant/house chart requires a place label and checked coordinates. A production system must add geocoding and chart uncertainty, exact transit change timestamps, a licensed ephemeris route, rule conflict handling and much broader verified source coverage.

Rule records cite `(slug, pdf_page, chapter, sloka)`. The enabled Phaladeepika XXVI excerpts were checked against the original scan's PDF pages 330, 331 and 332, not OCR alone. Four narrow examples are enabled. Rule text is a paraphrase and does not claim that Balaji himself applies that verse. No arbitrary interpretations are filled in. Jupiter fifth-from-Moon produces the children-related *traditional reading* from XXVI.19; it does **not** infer a child's marriage. No unsupported detail is promoted to a rule.

The benchmark's seven atomic rows are a tiny transcript-agreement smoke test. It checks four chart positions and three outcome claims. `chart_match` is agreement on calculated positions, not correct prediction; `unsupported` is a missing book-grounded outcome, not a disproved historical event. Both matches and gaps are printed. Evaluation against actual outcomes requires prospective, complete, timestamped forecasts with recorded misses and independently checked events.

## Natal geometry and period research slice

The optional `--birth-place 'Chennai, India' --birth-lat 13.0827 --birth-lon 80.2707` inputs add a Lahiri sidereal ascendant and whole-sign houses to the normal forecast JSON. **The supplied coordinates must be checked against the actual birth place; the name is a label, not a geocoder.** True birth clock time and IANA timezone remain mandatory. A nonexistent or ambiguous DST clock time is rejected rather than silently assigned an instant. Latitude is strictly between -90 and 90 degrees. The astronomical ascendant comes from Swiss Ephemeris `houses_ex` (sidereal, Lahiri); the returned house numbers are whole-sign from the rising sign, not Placidus or house cusps. Moshier's ephemeris is a research approximation; errors in time or coordinates may change the rising sign, especially near a sign boundary.

The natal JSON also contains a Moon-star period sequence. [Phaladeepika XIX.2-3](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.92117), original scan PDF p. 229 (printed p. 192), lists the nine star lords and their periods and specifies a fractional balance at birth. p. 230 (printed p. 193), XIX.4, defines a solar year by a return of the Sun to its natal longitude. Our 27 equal sidereal sectors and linear fractional balance are labeled computational approximations; the output is **solar-year offsets**, not fabricated calendar dates. The nine complete subperiods use the 120-year proportions described in B. Suryanarain Row's *Astrological Self-Instructor*, scan [source](https://archive.org/details/Astrology_Books_by_B_Suryanarayana_Row), PDF pp. 111-112 (printed pp. 97-98). These source pages were inspected as images. No personal outcome is implied by the period lord alone, and approximate offsets must not be presented as exact dates. Birth input/output should be handled as sensitive personal data in any product.

## Timed sign changes and star-pada audience (research)

`python3 -m engine.timing --planet Jupiter --start '2026-01-01T00:00:00+00:00' --end '2027-01-01T00:00:00+00:00'` scans a half-open UTC interval up to 366 days and returns Lahiri sidereal sign crossings and zero-longitude-speed stations. The root is bracketed to a second by Swiss Ephemeris/Moshier calculations, but this is a **numerical tolerance**, not an astronomical accuracy guarantee. It uses mean Rahu. Every result is model/ayanamsha-dependent; do not transfer published transit dates from videos to this model without recalculation. Use step size at most 12 hours; this sampling cannot certify undiscovered paired transitions inside a step. The Swiss Ephemeris [programming manual](https://www.astro.com/swisseph/swephprg.htm) defines `swe_calc_ut` as Universal Time input and `SEFLG_SPEED` for longitude speed. The library license gate still applies.

A sign-only forecast lists the nine possible Moon nakshatra padas as **audience labels**, without selecting a person's actual birth star or issuing star-specific outcomes. With real birth date/time/timezone, the CLI also reports the natal Moon's exact sector and pada. The 27-star sequence and four padas per star were visually verified in B. Suryanarain Row's *Astrological Self-Instructor* PDF p. 86 (printed p. 72), and nine padas per sign on PDF p. 88 (printed p. 74), [source scan](https://archive.org/details/Astrology_Books_by_B_Suryanarayana_Row). Equal sidereal sector geometry is the chosen calculation convention, not a claim that the book supplied modern ephemeris coordinates or Balaji's full star-specific interpretation.

The sign-lord list is visually verified against [Phaladeepika I.6, PDF p. 40](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.92117) (printed p. 3). The full seventh aspect and Saturn third/tenth, Jupiter fifth/ninth, Mars fourth/eighth are on II.23, PDF p. 55 (printed p. 18); IV.9, PDF p. 74 (printed p. 37) records different views on the strength of special aspects. Topical house labels are cited to XV.17, PDF p. 196 (printed p. 159), and the second/wealth reference to XV.20, PDF p. 197 (printed p. 160). All four source pages were visually checked. The structure yields, for example, Leo-reference Saturn in Pisces (8th) aspect targets: work-house 10, wealth-house 2 and children-house 5; it does **not** infer losses, job change or family events. A factor only becomes a finding when a separate, fully conditioned, checked rule supports that exact outcome. Source disagreements and missing timing/strength remain explicit.

The search for an individual-reading corpus is tracked in [the source-hunt note](../docs/balaji-individual-reading-source-hunt.md). No qualified full one-person recording was found in the searched public material, so this structural engine is not scored on individual predictive accuracy.

## Partial dignity, yoga, exchange, and three-level dasha (research)

`engine/natal_factors.py` emits original-scan-checked, **unadjusted candidate** sign-position fractions from [Phaladeepika IV.7](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.92117), PDF p. 73 (printed p. 36): exaltation sign 1 rupa, the precise moolatrikona portion 3/4, own sign 1/2, and fall sign 0. Sign ownership/exaltation and moolatrikona degree ranges are on I.6-7, PDF pp. 40-41. Overlaps (such as Mercury's Virgo exaltation, ownership, and moolatrikona) produce several candidates with `candidate_conflict`, **not a chosen score**. Automatic combustion, complete sixfold aggregation from IV.1 (PDF pp. 70-71), and global outcome precedence remain unimplemented. Later sections describe natural friendship and partial source-specific components. Nodes have no assigned fractions. Do not compare candidates as total strength or unlock XV.25 with them.

`maha_purusha_conditions` checks only the five planet/Kendra/own-or-exalted-sign conditions named in VI.1, PDF pp. 83-84 (printed pp. 46-47). `sign_exchanges` lists distinct mutual sign-owner pairs from VI.32, PDF p. 96 (printed p. 59); it does not classify the house-specific Dainya, Khala or Maha exchange categories. These flags are not promised outcomes. Their assignment from a supplied birth chart inherits the ascendant and coordinate uncertainty above.

`dasha_at_solar_offset(moon_longitude, offset)` identifies the major, subperiod, and third-level lords at a finite, nonnegative **solar-year offset** after birth, using the repeated 120-year proportions described in [*Astrological Self-Instructor*, PDF pp. 111-112](https://archive.org/details/Astrology_Books_by_B_Suryanarayana_Row) (printed pp. 97-98). It handles a partial first major period by calculating the earlier theoretical start before birth, then clipping the visible start to birth. Each interval is half-open and a boundary belongs to its new lord. This offset helper does not date periods on the civil calendar or infer an outcome; the separate solar_dates module supplies labeled convention-dependent estimates. Conflicts among rules still have no verified precedence; the engine declines to synthesize outcomes from these factors.

`engine/precedence.py` groups supplied candidate outcome claims by topic and interval and marks opposed polarities as a conflict. It also lists missing citations, strength and timing verification. Even agreement currently `abstain`s, since there is no page-checked book priority or independent outcome calibration. This is an explicit *absence* of a precedence rule, not an invented weighting policy; the existing transit verse findings remain separately labeled traditional readings rather than reconciled personal predictions.

## Solar-return calendar research: exact roots, estimated dasha dates

`python3 -m engine.solar_dates --birth-date 2000-01-01 --birth-time 14:30 --birth-tz Asia/Kolkata --at '2026-01-01T00:00:00+00:00'` emits three-level period intervals with UTC **estimates**. `SolarCalendar.annual_return(n)` solves the Lahiri/Moshier Sun's return to its natal sidereal longitude, including returns before birth, within a numerical bracket of 0.1 seconds. That tolerance is not an astronomical accuracy guarantee. Offsets are bounded to +/-150 years.

[Phaladeepika XIX.4, PDF p. 230 (printed p. 193)](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.92117), inspected again as a page image, defines a solar year by return to the birth Sun position and says to subdivide it. It does not uniquely define a civil mapping for fractional years. This module **explicitly chooses linear elapsed-UTC interpolation between adjacent annual returns**, rather than pretending that a fixed 365-day or 360-day year was specified. Return roots are model-exact within tolerance; the interpolated dasha dates are convention-dependent estimates. By default the initial Moon balance uses the equal-sector longitude approximation; the explicit measured-traversal options described below preserve alternative source profiles. Neither the calendar mapping nor initial balance has been identified as Balaji's own convention. These estimates do not satisfy the `timing_verified` outcome gate or enable disabled personal-outcome rules.
