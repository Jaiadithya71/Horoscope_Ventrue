# What People Actually Ask Astrologers - demand research

Compiled 2026-11-09. Documents the questions people most ask on astrology
platforms, from the platforms' own published data, as the agreed basis for
this project's next content and product steps. No direction is chosen in
this document; it maps demand so the decision works from evidence.

## How to read this

- Tier 1 = measured question counts published by the platforms themselves.
- Tier 2 = press interviews with founders quoting their internal tagging.
- Tier 3 = editorial "questions to ask an astrologer" listicles. These are
  marketing content, NOT demand evidence, and are quarantined at the end.

### Denominators differ - do not compare or pool

Each percentage below is a different base: Astrotalk's 65% is the founder's
daily-query tagging; the ~60% is January 2026 queries; the ~25% is India's
share of 12 million consultations; Om.AI counts typed FIRST questions by new
users; GrahAI counts questions touching a topic (multi-label, so its shares
sum past 100% and 39% of questions matched no topic). The figures are shown
side by side as separate measurements, never pooled.

### Main limitation

All Tier 1 data is self-published by the platforms, with opaque or
keyword-based methodology, and covers people who ask astrologers online -
not everyone. Read this as directional demand signals from several
independent platforms, not a representative industry ranking. Wording
examples are the phrasings the cited reports quote or describe, not lifted
user messages.

## The big pattern (measured)

### 1. Marriage and relationships - first in every dataset we found

Each dataset measures it differently (percentages not interchangeable - see
above).

- Astrotalk (founder interview, Aug 2025): "About 65% of our queries per day
  are on marriage and relationships" - concentrated in ages 25-35.
- Astrotalk (January 2026 queries): love and relationships nearly 60% of
  user concerns.
- Astrotalk 12M-consultation study (Apr 2025 - Mar 2026): in India,
  relationships + marriage nearly 25% of all consultations; the Indian
  questions are specifically when they will get married, with whom, and how
  the marriage will unfold.
- Om.AI (28,919 users, 157,974 questions, Jul-Oct 2026): marriage is 32% of
  typed first questions, 44% once love/partner questions are added.
- GrahAI (10,432 typed questions, Apr-Oct 2026): marriage and relationships
  touch 29.4% of questions.
- Melooha and Coto (founders, Aug 2025): relationships are their largest
  category too, including post-marriage problems, breakups and divorce.

### 2. Career and work - solid #2, and rising fast

- Astrotalk year-end 2025: career + relationship queries together were
  nearly 95% of all questions; job-related queries grew 50% year on year;
  "Is AI going to take my job?" was the platform's most frequently asked
  question of 2025.
- Astrotalk 12M study: career/business nearly 18% of Indian consultations.
- Astrotalk January 2026: career/financial anxieties ~28% of questions;
  "When will I get a job?" among the most common.
- GrahAI: career 20.9%; 14.8% of career questions mention government jobs
  (sarkari naukri, UPSC/SSC/bank/railway exams).
- Om.AI: 1,383 people asked about a government job - almost exactly as many
  as asked about an ex (1,464).

### 3. After the big two, single digits

GrahAI topic shares after marriage and career: the year ahead 9.7%,
education and exams 4.8%, money 4.7%, health 2.2%, children 2.0%, settling
abroad 1.9%, planetary transits 1.9%, dasha periods 1.6%, remedies 1.3%.
Kundli.me finds money is a steady 3-7% undercurrent at every age rather
than a headline - people ask about the life area carrying the money (job,
marriage, home), not wealth in the abstract.

## The questions, in the shapes people ask them

- Timing of marriage: "When will I get married?" - among the most common
  Astrotalk questions in 2025; 54.9% of Om.AI marriage questions and 34.8%
  of GrahAI marriage questions ask when, not what.
- Who and how: "Will I marry my partner?"; love marriage vs arranged
  marriage (10.8% of GrahAI marriage questions).
- The ex: Om.AI - 1,464 people asked whether an ex would come back or a
  breakup could be patched up; 26% of all love questions are about an ex.
- Jobs: "When will I get a job?"; "Is AI going to take my job?";
  government-job timing.
- Children: "When will we have a child?" - 77.7% of Om.AI children questions
  ask when; children come up four times as often in Hinglish (7.4%) as in
  English (1.8%).
- Home and abroad: when will I buy/build a home (70.8% of Om.AI home
  questions ask when); settling abroad a steady ~2%.

## Patterns behind the questions

- People ask "when", not "what". Om.AI: 42.6% of first questions ask for a
  time. GrahAI: 25.2% of all questions ask when / which year / at what age /
  kab tak. People arrive with the decision already in front of them and
  want a date or a window.
- The life-stage curve. Kundli.me (630 age-placed questions): ages 20-25
  ask 67% marriage vs 9% career; 26-32 is nearly level (45% vs 38%); 33-45
  flips to career leading (35% vs 25%); 46-59 is 64% career (small band,
  directional). Astrotalk sees the same: 18-24 asks about jobs, 25-34 about
  marriage, 35-45 about marital stability and career sustainability. Health
  questions rise with age (about 1% in the 20s, 8-11% after the mid-30s).
- Language changes the worry. Om.AI: in Hinglish, 48% of first questions
  are about marriage vs 29% in English; love and ex questions run higher in
  English (13.7% vs 7.0%). GrahAI: 60.1% of questions from the Tamil
  interface were typed in Tamil script - a Tamil-first surface meets people
  in their own words.
- Late-night asking. Om.AI: 15.6% of askers asked at least once between
  midnight and 4am IST; midnight was as busy as 11pm. These are worry
  questions asked in the moment, not planned research.

## Relevance to this project

Interpretation, not direction:

- The dominant question shape is timing ("when"), and dasha-bhukti timing
  is precisely what this engine already computes from sourced classical
  rules - a fit between documented demand and existing capability.
- Content hooks that mirror the questions people actually type ("when will
  I marry", "will I marry my partner", "when will I get a job", love vs
  arranged) start from documented demand rather than guesses.
- Any use of this keeps the project's existing guardrails: belief framing,
  no guaranteed-result promises, no remedy how-to.
